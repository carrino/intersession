# What to bring

One bag on the train, so this is the short list. Low voltage only: the big bench supply
stays home. Check with the maker space ahead of time about what they have (bottom).

## Boards and parts

- All eight CLUE boards: one each for the four students and me, three spares.
- The Adafruit order: sensors, STEMMA QT cables, battery holders, encoders, cap touch,
  haptic drivers and motor discs, alligator clips, conductive fabric, flux pen.
- Micro-USB data cables, one per student plus spares, each one tested and marked. A
  charge-only cable cost half an hour on day one.
- AAA batteries: three per holder plus a spare set.
- A little kit: resistors, a few LEDs, jumper wires, a half-size breadboard or two.
- Double-sided foam tape, velcro, zip ties, rubber bands: how projects get held together.
  The CLUE has no mounting holes; the only holes are the five ring pads on its bottom
  edge, which are live pins, so only nylon screws go through those.
- A roll of 6 mm Kapton tape, in case a board still jams when touched: a strip over the
  small gold fingers between the big rings covers the I2C lines that run out there.

## Tools

- Soldering iron with stand and tip cleaner, lead-free solder. Flux pen is in the order.
- Flush cutters, wire strippers, small pliers, tweezers, small screwdrivers.
- Multimeter.
- Moku with its power supply and probes, for the scope and signal generator demos.
- Safety glasses for whoever is at the iron.

## Computer and demo

- Laptop and charger, with the Arduino IDE, the nRF52 board package, the Arcada
  "install all" and every sensor's library already installed and tested against a board.
- A USB stick with the IDE installers and the board package, for the student whose
  download fails. The board package alone is a big download on shared wifi.
- Projector adapter (HDMI, USB-C), and the slides: quaternions, PCBs.
- TechDisc, charged, with the phone and app for the demo.
- Phone with the Bluefruit Connect app, as a second way to poke at a board over BLE.

## Ask the maker space beforehand

- 3D printers and filament, for project housings. Bring a few STL files ready to go.
- Soldering stations and a bench supply, so the iron and Moku can stay light.
- Wifi that can handle a room of IDE downloads, enough power strips, and how the
  projector connects.

## Leaving at home

- The big power supply.
